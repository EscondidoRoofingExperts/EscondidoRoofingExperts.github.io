Escondido Tile Roof FAQ

Clay and concrete tile roofs are everywhere in Escondido, from older Spanish-style homes near downtown to newer neighborhoods in the hills. These are short answers to the questions homeowners ask most often about them.

My tiles look fine. Why is the roof leaking?

Tiles shed most of the rain, but the underlayment beneath them is the layer that actually keeps water out. Underlayment ages much faster than tile, so on a roof that is 20 or more years old, leaks often come from worn underlayment even when every tile is intact. More detail: tile roof underlayment explained.

Can I walk on my tile roof to check it?

It is best not to. Clay and concrete tiles crack under uneven weight, and a single misstep can create the leak you were looking for. Check from the ground with binoculars and leave roof walking to a professional who knows where to step.

What is a "lift and relay"?

When underlayment fails, a roofer can remove the tiles, install new underlayment (and battens if needed), then put the original tiles back, replacing only the broken ones. It usually costs less than a full re-roof with new tile.

Should I repair or replace?

A few cracked or slipped tiles are a repair. Leaks in several places, sagging areas or widespread underlayment failure point toward a lift and relay or replacement. This repair-or-replace checker walks through the signs.

Do I need a permit?

Re-roofing in Escondido generally requires a City of Escondido building permit, and California's Title 24 energy rules can affect material choices on some re-roofs. Your contractor should know who pulls the permit.

How do I know a roofer is legitimate?

In California, roofing work of $1,000 or more must be done by a contractor licensed by the Contractors State License Board, usually with a C-39 Roofing license. Ask for the license number and look it up for free at cslb.ca.gov. A checklist of questions to ask: how to choose a roofer in Escondido.
